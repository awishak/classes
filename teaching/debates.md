# Ethical debates

Three questions per topic, one of each kind. Put all three on the board, let students pick, split into three groups, then come back together and hear all three out. The format seed lives in [seeds.md](seeds.md) under "Should / Could / Would."

The three kinds:

- **Should** — what ought to happen. The policy argument.
- **Could** — a prediction. Where does this go, and how fast.
- **Would** — personal. If the chance landed in front of you, what do you do.

Sources are named, not link-checked. Verify before they go on a slide.

---

## COMM 118 — Sports Communication

### Week 1 — Gambling (Purpose of Sports)

**Should.** Should universities be allowed to take money from sportsbooks?

- The New York Times investigated campus-sportsbook partnerships in 2022; schools like Michigan State, LSU, and Colorado had signed deals, several of which paid the school per student who opened an account.
- The American Gaming Association revised its Responsible Marketing Code in 2023 to bar partnerships with colleges except for responsible-gaming messaging.

**Could.** In ten years, will a major American league own a sportsbook outright instead of just partnering with one?

- The NBA made MGM its first official betting partner in 2018, months after *Murphy v. NCAA*. Every league followed.
- American Gaming Association annual revenue reports track how fast the money grew.

**Would.** You run social media for a team. You know the star is sitting tonight and it hasn't been announced. Nobody would ever find out. Would you bet it?

- Jontay Porter got a lifetime NBA ban in April 2024 for exactly this shape of thing.
- The NCAA's 2024 report on betting-related abuse aimed at college athletes.

### Week 2 — Value & Interest

**Should.** Should a league change its rules to make the sport more entertaining, even if purists say it's now a worse sport?

- MLB's pitch clock, bigger bases, and shift ban, all in 2023. Game times dropped by about half an hour.
- The NBA's play-in tournament and the In-Season Tournament.

**Could.** Will the most-watched competition among American 18-to-24-year-olds in 2035 be a video game?

- Look at viewership numbers for League of Legends Worlds against a typical NBA Finals game.
- Any recent Nielsen or SBJ breakdown of the median age of each league's TV audience. Baseball's is the number that shocks students.

**Would.** You work in league marketing. You're asked to push a rule change you personally think makes the sport worse but the ratings data says will work. Would you write the pitch?

- Bam Adebayo's 83-point game is the seed for this one; the farce was more fun than the real thing precisely because it was rare.

### Week 3 — Athletes, Celebrity, and Corporations

**Should.** Should every NIL deal a college athlete signs be public?

- The House v. NCAA settlement, approved in June 2025, set up a clearinghouse to review third-party NIL deals over a certain dollar amount.
- Compare with how pro contracts work: NFL and NBA salaries are public, endorsement deals mostly aren't.

**Could.** Will athlete-owned media put traditional sports media out of business?

- Pat McAfee's move to ESPN, and what he kept control of.
- *New Heights*, *Mind the Game*, and what happened to talk radio ratings.

**Would.** A company offers you $50,000 to post about a product you've never used and don't believe in. Your following trusts you. Would you take it?

- Any FTC endorsement-guide enforcement action; the rules on disclosing paid promotion.
- Parasocial trust is the concept underneath this, which is where the week lands anyway.

### Week 4 — Media Rights and Spectacle

**Should.** Should the biggest games be allowed to go behind a paywall, or does the public have a claim on them?

- The UK's "listed events" rule keeps the World Cup and the Olympics on free-to-air television. Australia has a similar anti-siphoning law.
- The US has nothing like this.

**Could.** Will the Super Bowl be streaming-only within ten years?

- Peacock's exclusive NFL playoff game in January 2024, and its viewership number.
- Netflix's Christmas Day NFL games in 2024 and the Paul-Tyson fight.

**Would.** You're cutting a documentary. The footage doesn't support the heroic version of your subject, but the heroic version is the better film. Would you cut it that way?

- *Last Dance* and the debate over Jordan's editorial control.
- Sets up the OJ week nicely.

### Week 5 — Fans, Identity, Gender, Race, Politics

**Should.** Should a league be allowed to restrict what athletes say or wear about politics?

- Olympic Charter Rule 50, and how the IOC softened it before Tokyo.
- The NBA bubble in 2020: approved jersey messages, from a list.

**Could.** Will an active star player in a major American men's league come out in the next ten years?

- Carl Nassib in 2021, Jason Collins in 2013, and what happened to each career afterward.
- Compare with women's leagues, where the answer is obviously different, and ask why.

**Would.** Your favorite team's owner funds something you find repugnant. Would you stop watching?

- Donald Sterling and the 2014 Clippers.
- Ask what "stop watching" even costs the owner. That's the better half of the discussion.

### Week 6 — Civic Pride, Stadiums, the NCAA

**Should.** Should cities be allowed to put public money into stadiums?

- Bradbury, Coates, and Humphreys, "The Impact of Sports Stadiums on Localized Economic Activity," Journal of Economic Surveys, 2023. It reviews decades of research and finds the subsidies exceed any measurable local benefit.
- The Buffalo Bills deal in 2022, roughly $850 million in public money.

**Could.** Will college athletes be employees within ten years?

- House v. NCAA, approved June 2025, with revenue sharing starting that school year.
- The Dartmouth men's basketball unionization vote in 2024, and Johnson v. NCAA in the Third Circuit.

**Would.** Would you let your twelve-year-old specialize in one sport year-round for a real shot at a scholarship?

- The Aspen Institute's Project Play reports on what youth sports cost families and when kids quit.
- Ask the room how many of them played year-round. That's the actual source.

### Week 7 — OJ: Made in America

**Should.** Should a criminal trial be broadcast live?

- Judge Ito's decision to allow cameras, and what the coverage did to the proceedings.
- Federal courts still ban cameras. Ask why the rules differ.

**Could.** Would a trial like that today be decided more on social media than in the courtroom?

- Compare with the Depp-Heard trial in 2022 and how it ran online.

**Would.** You're offered the job producing that coverage. It will make your career. Would you take it?

- This one usually splits the room hardest. Let it.

### Week 8 — Leadership and Culture

**Should.** Should a coach who wins be kept despite a culture people describe as abusive?

- Rutgers and Mike Rice in 2013, fired only after the practice tape aired.
- Northwestern and Pat Fitzgerald in 2023, fired over hazing after an initial two-week suspension.

**Could.** Will player empowerment end the head coach as the central authority in pro sports?

- Coaching tenure lengths now against thirty years ago.
- The rise of the player-driven roster move.

**Would.** You see your boss do something wrong. Reporting it ends your career in this industry, and you know that going in. Would you?

- Penn State: the assistants who saw and what each of them did.
- This is the one to run last, because it's the one they'll actually face.
