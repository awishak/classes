## The point of the day

Students walk out able to say what a team is in three layers, and to name
four things a team has to settle before the ball is in play: culture,
motivation, cohesion, and who plays what role.

The three layers:

1. **The individual.** The person on the roster. Sometimes a performer,
   sometimes a teammate. (His transcript cut out here; this line is my
   reading of "sometimes a performer but other times.")
2. **The team.** The people who have to coordinate to play.
3. **The organization.** The front office, the owner, the business side,
   the people who sell the tickets. The team sits inside the organization
   and the organization holds the team up. Or does not.

Culture runs through all three. His phrase: culture is the number one way
we talk about how sports teams act.

## The story: Knicks ring night

Joe Vardon, The Athletic, October 22, 2026, "Knicks coaches didn't get
rings on banner night; MSG staff received phishing email." Gift link:
https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7715834/2026/10/22/knicks-ring-night-assistant-coaches-msg-employees/?unlocked_article_code=1.K1E.uJ6P.Ed6K2PlxZOxp

The facts, in telling order:

- Tuesday October 20, Madison Square Garden. The Knicks raise the 2026
  championship banner and hand out 22-carat diamond rings from Jason of
  Beverly Hills. Leon Rose, the team president, opens his box first and
  the ring is there.
- The assistant coaches, in white pullovers, are each handed a black box
  with an orange stripe. None of them open the box. The boxes are empty.
  Seven league sources confirm the assistants got no ring that night.
- The assistants found out earlier that day. They had never been fitted.
  The empty boxes were a late addition to the ceremony. Reactions ran
  from annoyance to genuine anger.
- Whether head coach Mike Brown got his ring, The Athletic could not
  confirm. MSG Sports: "We are not disclosing anything about the rings."
- The assistants were told rings would come eventually. Many longtime MSG
  employees, including some in Knicks business operations, will get no
  ring at all. Most NBA teams give most employees a cheaper ring.
- Late September, those employees got an email: "Congratulations, your
  Employee Championship Ring is here," two designs to pick from, no cost.
  A phishing test. Anyone who clicked was sent to a mandatory
  cybersecurity class, to answer James Dolan's growing security worries.
- Also on the court: Roger Goodell stood behind Mike Breen and was on
  camera all night. Adam Silver sat at the far end, away from the ring
  table, and did not speak. Silver's office and Dolan have been at odds
  for years.
- For the record: the Thunder assistants did not get rings on opening
  night last season either. The Cavaliers' assistants did in 2016, the
  Lakers' in 2020. The timing alone is normal. The empty boxes and the
  employees who get nothing are the story.

## The questions for the room

His frame: is the organization helpful or not helpful to the team?
Every question below asks that question a different way.

1. **Who is on the team?** Players got rings. Coaches got boxes. Ticket
   staff got a phishing test. Draw MSG's line. Now draw
   your own line. That line is role clarity at the
   organizational level: who counts as a member and what a member gets.
2. **The empty box is a message.** Somebody decided the ceremony should
   look complete on TV. What did the coaches hear? What is the difference
   between "your rings are not ready yet" said out loud and a sealed box
   you know is empty, in front of 19,000 people?
3. **The phishing email.** A security test is normal at a big company.
   This one promised the thing the employees had just been told they
   would never get. What does that say about "the way things are done"
   at the Garden? Who signed off, and did anyone in the room say no?
   (Upward dissent, concept 7, for Nov 4.)
4. **Motivation.** The ring is the reward. If you are a Knicks assistant
   tonight, what are you playing for in game one? Is this a mastery
   climate or a performance climate, and does the coach or the owner
   set that climate?
5. **Cohesion.** Task cohesion on the floor can be fine while social
   cohesion between the bench and the front office is gone. Can a team
   keep winning inside an organization its coaches resent? For how long?
6. **Silver and Goodell.** The league commissioner sat silent at the
   champion's ring night while the other league's commissioner was on
   camera. The team sits inside the organization, and the organization
   sits inside the league. Who is the Knicks' organization helping?
7. **The Thunder comparison.** Their assistants waited too. So which
   detail makes the Knicks' night a culture story and the Thunder's a
   scheduling note?
