## The concept list

Twelve from the two chapters; he picks ten. Each one is a thing a student
can define in two sentences and attach an example to.

| # | Concept | The line from the chapter | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Coordination, physical and mental | Teams react to each other and to the field; it runs on talk, posture and signs. NBA teams that touched more early in the season won more (Kraus 2010). | 2021 p. 507; 2017 §5E |
| 2 | Knowledge sharing | "Action rules" like find open space. Tall teams (football) simplify and share explicit knowledge; flat teams (hockey) share tacit knowledge. | 2021 p. 508; 2017 §3F |
| 3 | Role clarity and role ambiguity | Role sender names the role, the athlete accepts or negotiates. Ambiguity breeds tension; clarity predicted performance. | 2021 p. 508; 2017 §3G |
| 4 | Goal setting | Process goals beat outcome goals; approach beats avoidance; rugby coaches move from detailed to general as the match nears. | 2021 p. 509; 2017 §3A |
| 5 | Motivational climate | Mastery (task-involving) against performance (ego-involving). Five principles of a mastery climate. | 2021 p. 509; 2017 §3C |
| 6 | Culture | "The way things are done." What coaches and players say and do daily makes the culture; culture change in Olympic and rugby teams. | 2021 p. 510; 2017 §4C |
| 7 | Conflict and dissent | Communication breakdown as the main source. Upward dissent when the coach's feedback style is open, lateral dissent to teammates when closed. Effective dissent: a solution, direct facts, no humor. | 2021 p. 510; 2017 §4E |
| 8 | Cohesion | Task and social. Coaching behaviors that build it (Turman's list: teasing, speeches, a respected opponent, athlete-led events). The downside of too much. | 2021 p. 511; 2017 §4B |
| 9 | Power | French and Raven's five bases. Athletes are happier under reward, referent and expert power; coercive power makes them flinch at feedback. | 2017 §4D |
| 10 | Coaching as complexity | "The edge of chaos." The five dimensions of the Leadership Scale for Sports; immediacy predicts satisfaction and cohesion. | 2021 p. 506; 2017 §4A |
| 11 | Emotion and time | Choking; coaches regulate emotion through culture; Dweck's mindset. Time is epochal: teams plan backward from the game and talk about scenarios in advance. Time-outs. | 2021 p. 512; 2017 §5A, §5D |
| 12 | Sports as a model for work | Baseball is pooled, football sequential, basketball reciprocal (Keidel and Katz). Do not confuse coaching with managing. Business is not "in bounds." | 2017 §7 |

Concept 12 is the bridge to week 9. If he cuts to ten, 1 and 11 are the
ones that overlap most with the rest.

## The one assignment

Keep Weekly Challenge 3, the former coach interview, due Sunday, November
1, and point it at the concepts. The student picks four concepts from the
list and interviews a former coach with one question per concept, then
writes a page: what the coach said, and which concept it is an example of.
That gives every student four live examples for the memo a week later, and
Sit-Down 2 can start with "tell me what your coach said about dissent."

If he would rather have the assignment be the topic-choosing one (the
Worktopia shape), the worksheet is: rank the ten concepts by how much you
have seen each one on a team you were on, write the example for your top
three, and name the one you want to be quizzed on. Due the same Sunday.

## Sit-Down 2

**The memo, due Sunday, November 8.** One Google Doc shared to SCU. For
each of the ten concepts: a two-sentence definition in their own words and
one example, from a team they were on, a team they follow, or the coach
interview. Then a half page on their one concept: why this one, and the
best example they have. Two pages.

**The room.** Individual, in Vari 206 like Sit-Down 1, on audio. The math:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Students | 25 |
| Class minutes, Nov 11 and Nov 13 | 130 |
| Minutes each with no gaps | 5.2 |

Five minutes is a quiz, not a conversation. Two ways to get to eight:
use Thursday, November 12 for a third block of slots, or run Monday,
November 9 (now a no-meeting day) as sign-up slots in his office. With
three blocks, eight minutes each.

**The eight minutes.** Two concepts drawn at random from the ten (define
it, give me an example that is not in your memo), then their one concept
(the three questions below), then one question that points at week 9:
which job from Sit-Down 1 does this concept matter most for, and why.

**Three questions for each concept**, for the instructor sheet:
- What is the difference between the two kinds (task and social cohesion,
  mastery and performance climate, upward and lateral dissent, explicit and
  tacit knowledge, process and outcome goals)?
- Give me a team where this went wrong. What was said, by whom?
- What would you do about it as the coach? As a player with no title?

The sheet for his side can be built the way the Sit-Down 1 sheet was: one
card per student from the memo, the examples they wrote, and the three
questions for their concept.
