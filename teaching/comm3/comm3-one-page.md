**COMM 3, Digital Storytelling. Fall 2026.**

Lower division, about 35 students, mostly first years. Ten weeks starting Monday, September 21, meeting Monday, Wednesday, and Friday. No class Thanksgiving week. Final due Wednesday, December 9.

**Course objectives**

1. Identify and understand basic narrative elements.
2. Identify a target audience and the best platform or distribution channel to connect with the audience.
3. Critically analyze the power and privilege of content creators.
4. Create research-based social justice stories using an audio story, a short video, and a multimedia project.

**Approach**

I use stories to structure class time. Rather than presenting a concept and then illustrating it, I present the stories first and we work out the concept from them. Some are major news stories. Some are local or from campus, since a character can be someone everyone on campus knows and a story can be something SCU is doing. Students then apply what they learned by making something of their own.

I want two things by the end of the quarter. First, when students read a story, whether it is about an athlete or a political event, they understand how the medium, the viewpoint, and the decisions behind the telling shape what they are reading. Second, and more important, they can do it themselves. Podcasts, video, and multimedia are the forms we use, but the underlying point is that whenever anyone tells a story, the choice of medium, what gets included, and the point of view all matter.

Each week is organized around one skill and one question.

1. Week 1, September 21. Identifying stories and framing. What makes something a story, and how does the same event become different stories depending on who is telling it?
2. Week 2, September 28. Visual language. How do angle, distance, framing, and editing change what a photograph or a clip says?
3. Week 3, October 5. Graphics. What can a graphic show that words cannot, and how do graphics mislead? Visual story pre-production due Friday.
4. Week 4, October 12. Narrative arc. What are the parts of a story, and how does a deadline turn events into one? Visual story due Sunday.
5. Week 5, October 19. Characters. What turns a person into a character, and who decides who the hero is?
6. Week 6, October 26. Audio quality. How does the recording itself change what people hear, and what do we owe the people we record? No class Monday; students work on their treatments. Audio story pre-production due Friday.
7. Week 7, November 2. Audience and platform. How do you identify who a story is for, and what happens when a different audience finds it? Audio story due Sunday.
8. Week 8, November 9. Final project overview, story options, group formation, and how to shoot video.
9. Week 9, November 16. Groups pitch their stories for approval. Two weeks of production follow, including Thanksgiving. No class November 18 or 20.
10. Week 10, November 30. How to edit video. On Wednesday each group presents its story to the class and submits pre-production that night. Friday is a workshop. Final due December 9.

**Feedback**

On Fridays I am in the classroom and students come by to talk through their work. Feedback is given in person rather than in writing. Office hours are Thursdays.

**Grading**

1. Creative exercises, 15 percent. Five short individual exercises, due Sundays in weeks 1, 2, 3, 5, and 6. Each applies the week's skill to something from the student's own life and something from the world. They are low stakes individually, but a student who does not complete them will not do well in the course.
2. Visual story, 20 percent. Pairs. About 500 words, four or five photographs, and two or three graphics, interspersed. Due October 18.
3. Audio story, 20 percent. Pairs. Due November 8.
4. Final project, 35 percent. Groups of four. Video, audio, and multimedia together. Due December 9.
5. Class culture, 10 percent.

Each of the three projects has a pre-production step nine days before it is due. The treatment is part of it: what the story is, why, and a few sources. I approve it or send it back.

I am using whole letter grades this year, A, B, C, with no pluses or minuses and no percentages shown to students. At the end of the quarter I look at the full set of grades for both the average and for growth.

Students use whatever equipment they have, and a phone is fine. Strong photography can earn extra credit. They may use AI to produce graphics, with the tradeoff that they get less control. I will show them Flourish as the alternative.
