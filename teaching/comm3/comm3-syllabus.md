**Course description**

I use stories to structure class time. Rather than presenting a concept and then illustrating it, I present the stories first and we work out the concept from them. Some are major news stories. Some are local or from campus, since a character can be someone everyone on campus knows and a story can be something SCU is doing. Students then apply what they learned by making something of their own.

I want two things by the end of the quarter. First, when students read a story, whether it is about an athlete or a political event, they understand how the medium, the viewpoint, and the decisions behind the telling shape what they are reading. Second, and more important, they can do it themselves. Podcasts, video, and multimedia are the forms we use, but the underlying point is that whenever anyone tells a story, the choice of medium, what gets included, and the point of view all matter.

Exercises are due Sundays. Pre-production for the visual and audio stories is due the Friday nine days before the project. Pre-production for the final is due the night of the group presentations, Wednesday, December 2. Pre-production counts toward the project it belongs to, not toward the creative exercise grade.

Creative exercises are always individual. The visual story and the audio story are done in pairs. The final project is done in groups of four.

Each piece gets a whole letter grade. There are no pluses or minuses on individual assignments and students do not see percentages. The final course grade is built from the letters lined up together, read for the average and for growth across the quarter.

## Page 3. Objectives

**Department Learning Outcomes**

The course objectives meet the following Department Learning Outcomes:

- LO 1: Explain and apply communication theories and concepts.
- LO 3: Analyze communication structures that maintain power and privilege and engage in practices that support diversity, equity, and inclusion.
- LO 4: Analyze the ethical dimensions of communication and form judgments and practices based on evidence and values.
- LO 6: Create effective and ethical oral, visual, and written communication based on awareness of diverse perspectives, contexts, and social identities.
- LO 7: Design communication that fosters a more just, humane, or sustainable world.

**Course objectives**

1. Identify and understand basic narrative elements. (LO 1)
2. Identify a target audience and the best platform or distribution channel to connect with the audience. (LO 1)
3. Critically analyze the power and privilege of content creators. (LO 3, 4)
4. Create research-based social justice stories using an audio story, a short video, and a multimedia project. (LO 6, 7)

**Late Work**

Over many years of teaching, I have gone through a number of different policies regarding late work. What I've found is that the best policy is to be consistent, clear, and simple. This might seem strict to you, but again, this is what I've found to work the best.

If an assignment is not turned in at the deadline, the student will receive an F for that assignment. This includes turning in the wrong assignment, a mostly-incomplete assignment, or an assignment that doesn't meet basic requirements.

Then, the regrade policy applies. So the best a late assignment can earn is a D.

**Regrading**

If you would like to earn a higher grade, you may re-submit your assignment within 48 hours of receiving your grade. A resubmission can raise your grade by one letter.

Some assignments will not have improvement opportunities, including those submitted during Finals Week, and presentations. Please keep that in mind as we make our way towards the end of the quarter.

**Plagiarism**

Plagiarism and cheating in any form is unacceptable in this course, as it is in any course at SCU. There are no excuses. If you plagiarize or are found to be academically dishonest in any way, you will receive a grade of zero on that assignment. For incidents that I consider to be exceptional, you will receive a failing grade for the course. We'll talk about what counts as plagiarism, but if you're unsure in the meantime, come talk to me, or see https://www.scu.edu/academic-integrity/.

**AI**

We'll have a conversation about AI in class. Otherwise, my expectation is that we will use AI to assist us when I call it appropriate.
