**COMM 3, Digital Storytelling. Course plan as of September 8, 2026.**

This is where the course stands right now. Some pieces are locked and some are still being decided. The open items are at the end.

**The course**

COMM 3 is a lower division course with about 35 students, most of them first years. It runs ten weeks, starting Monday, September 21, and meets Monday, Wednesday, and Friday. Thanksgiving week is off. The final is due Wednesday, December 9. One meeting each week is a workshop day, and some of those will not be in person. I hold office hours on Thursdays. On Fridays I am in the classroom and students come by to talk through their work. Feedback happens in those conversations rather than in writing.

The course has four learning outcomes.

1. Identify and understand basic narrative elements.
2. Identify a target audience and the platform or channel best suited to reach it.
3. Critically analyze the power and privilege of content creators.
4. Create research-based social justice stories using an audio story, a short video, and a multimedia project.

There is no required topic list beyond those outcomes. The stories we use in class to teach each skill do not have to be social justice stories. That weight sits in the assignments students make themselves.

Stories in this class are not only news stories. A character can be someone who works on campus that everyone knows. A story can be something SCU is doing. Each week will include at least one local or personal example alongside the media stories, so students see the skill applied to their own world before they are asked to do it.

Students choose their own subjects, with my approval. They use whatever equipment they have, and a phone camera is fine. Strong photography can earn extra credit. There is no video assignment before the final because there is a separate film class.

**How the ten weeks run**

The first seven weeks are organized around skills, one per week except week 1, which covers identifying stories and framing together. Each of those weeks has a Sunday deadline for either a creative exercise on that skill or one of the two paired projects. The last three weeks are for the final project.

1. Week 1, September 21 to 25. Identifying stories and framing. Monday: what makes something a story at all? Wednesday: how does the same event become different stories? Friday: workshop for the exercise. Exercise due Sunday, September 27.
2. Week 2, September 28 to October 2. Visual language. Monday: how do angle, distance, framing, and treatment change what a photograph says? Wednesday: how does editing change what we think we saw? Friday: workshop for the exercise. Exercise due Sunday, October 4.
3. Week 3, October 5 to 9. Graphics. Monday: what can a graphic show that words cannot? Wednesday: how do graphics mislead, and what do we owe the people reading them? Friday: workshop for the visual story, and visual story pre-production due. Exercise due Sunday, October 11.
4. Week 4, October 12 to 16. Narrative arc. Monday: what are the parts of a story, and what order do they come in? Wednesday: how does a deadline turn events into a story? Friday: workshop for the visual story. Visual story due Sunday, October 18. No exercise.
5. Week 5, October 19 to 23. Characters. Monday: what turns a person into a character? Wednesday: who gets to decide who the hero and the villain are? Friday: workshop for the exercise. Exercise due Sunday, October 25.
6. Week 6, October 26 to 30. Audio quality. Monday: no class, students work on their audio story treatment. Wednesday: how does the recording itself change what people hear, and what do we owe the people we record? Friday: workshop for the audio story, and audio story pre-production due. Exercise due Sunday, November 1.
7. Week 7, November 2 to 6. Audience and platform. Monday: how do you identify the audience for a story? Wednesday: how does the platform change the story, and what happens when the wrong audience finds it? Friday: workshop for the audio story. Audio story due Sunday, November 8. No exercise.
8. Week 8, November 9 to 13. Final project. Monday: what is the final project, and what does it ask of you? Wednesday: what stories are worth the final, and groups form. Friday: how do you shoot video that tells a story? Confirm SCU meets Wednesday, November 11.
9. Week 9, November 16 to 20. Monday: groups pitch their story and get it approved or sent back. No class Wednesday or Friday; groups work on the final. Thanksgiving week off; groups keep working.
10. Week 10, November 30 to December 4. Monday: how do you edit video so the story comes through? Wednesday: groups present their story to the class, and the final pre-production document is due that night. Friday: workshop for the final. Final due Wednesday, December 9.

**Assignments and grading**

1. Creative exercises, 15 percent. Five of them, in weeks 1, 2, 3, 5, and 6. Always individual. Each one has students apply that week's skill to something from their own life and something from the world.
2. Visual story, 20 percent. Done in pairs. Around 500 words, four or five photographs, and two or three graphics, interspersed. Due Sunday, October 18.
3. Audio story, 20 percent. Done in pairs. Modeled on the audio story assignment in Lotta's version of the course. Due Sunday, November 8.
4. Final project, 35 percent. Done in groups of four. Combines video, audio, and multimedia. Due Wednesday, December 9.
5. Class culture, 10 percent.

The visual and audio stories each have a pre-production component due the Friday nine days before the project. The final's pre-production is due the night of the group presentations, Wednesday, December 2. The treatment lives inside pre-production. A treatment says what the story is, why, and lists a few sources. I approve it or send it back. Pre-production counts toward the project it belongs to, not toward the creative exercise grade.

Each piece gets a whole letter grade. There are no pluses or minuses on individual assignments and students do not see percentages. The final course grade is built from the letters lined up together, read for the average and for growth across the quarter.

**The stories we teach from**

I have rated a bank of stories to anchor each skill week. The ones below are the ones I rated highest. Two per teaching day is about what a week can hold, so most weeks have more than they need and the rest is a bench. Local and campus examples are still to be added for every week.

1. Identifying stories and framing: the Sycamore Gap tree, the Hurricane Katrina photo captions, and how different outlets frame the same Trump action as good or bad. On the bench: the Willie Horton ad, Fyre Festival, Covington Catholic, Tonya Harding, and the Menendez brothers.
2. Visual language: the O.J. Simpson Time cover, the Rodney King tape, the Exxon Valdez wildlife photographs, Astroworld, Free Solo, the Falling Man, NFL Films, Evan Vucci's raised fist photograph, and the Situation Room photograph. On the bench: the Los Angeles fires.
3. Graphics: Spotify Wrapped, the Theranos charts, the warming stripes, W.E.B. Du Bois at the Paris Exposition, John Snow's cholera map, The Pudding, the New York Times You Draw It format, the Strava heatmap, the hurricane cone of uncertainty, and the London Underground map. On the bench: the Ever Given, the Key Bridge collapse, the Challenger O-ring charts, the Boeing door plug, the Panama Papers, the redlining maps, and a unit on misleading charts.
4. Narrative arc: the Chilean miners, the Boston Marathon manhunt, Apollo 13, and the college admissions scandal. On the bench: the Thai cave rescue, Kendrick Lamar and Drake, the Titan submersible, Notre Dame, Hawk Tuah, the Deepwater Horizon spillcam, Baby Jessica, Free Britney, and Leicester City.
5. Characters: Gypsy Rose Blanchard, Richard Jewell, Steve Bartman, Balloon Boy, Elizabeth Holmes, Monica Lewinsky, and Colin Kaepernick. On the bench: the Biden debate.
6. Audio quality: the Dean Scream, the Astros trash can, Serial, the Zoom cat lawyer, found audio in true crime podcasts, and the Access Hollywood tape. On the bench: S-Town.
7. Audience and platform: Zohran Mamdani's campaign, the Bad Bunny halftime show, Duolingo on TikTok, Trump and Twitter, Nixon and Kennedy, AOC on Twitch, MrBeast's dubbing operation, and Bud Light. On the bench: New Coke, The Blair Witch Project, Wrexham, and Spanish-language local news in California.
